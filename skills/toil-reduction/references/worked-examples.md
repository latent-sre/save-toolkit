# Worked cost comparison

These fictional inputs demonstrate the cost boundary, not team thresholds or measured benefits.

| Input | Evidence or assumption |
|---|---|
| Baseline | 24 distinct interventions in one representative month, 15 minutes each including rework: 6 human-hours. Duplicate tickets/pages for an intervention count once. |
| Automated path | Estimated residual handling 2 h/month plus maintenance 2 h/month across all affected teams. |
| Build | Estimated 40 h including testing, migration and training. |
| Ownership | Application team builds; operations lead accepts the residual 4 h/month. |

Net saving is 2 h/month; time-only break-even is 20 months. The proposer supplied the estimates
without a comparable observed automation; in this example the operations lead accepts them
unadjusted. Maintenance rising to 3 h/month halves savings and doubles payback. With build effort
30–50 h and net savings 1–3 h/month, outer bounds are 10–50 months; dependence between estimates
can narrow that range.

Keep simplification as an alternative until the expected useful life and adoption pattern support
that horizon. Compare residual handling and maintenance after a month of comparable demand before
calling the benefit realized. Error or customer-impact reductions need separate evidence.

If the same 6-hour baseline becomes 3 hours of handling plus 4 hours of maintenance elsewhere,
net saving is **−1 h/month**, with no positive time-saving break-even. A demonstrated safety benefit
may still justify the change; transferred work cannot be counted as efficiency.
