## Rejected candidate — the fence-wording fix (`a9377d4a`, reverted)

`[verified]` One body candidate was built and measured against the same instrument before the
scenario was reclassified. It added, to slot 1 of the answer-shape blockquote, an explanation of
why a fence is a distinct output element and an instruction to write each command "in full, inline,
so a human can paste it" — aimed at the two observed failure modes (fencing, and abbreviating a
command to "the describe call"). Result on `a9377d4a`, same CLI 2.1.251, Sonnet, 3 trials, run
`20260829T213859Z-c3ab2ab7`: **0/3, with every non-fence grader passing 3/3** — both content
misses were genuinely cleared — but the fence clause failed in all three trials, against one of
three on the cut and two of three on the base.

Disposition: **reverted**, per `evals/README.md`'s failure-to-regression rule ("retain the
incumbent on a tie or non-comparable result"). The verdict tied at 0/3 while the sub-grader
detail moved in both directions, which is the non-comparable case.

The transferable lesson is why it backfired: **a rationale that motivates the forbidden behavior
teaches the behavior.** "So a human can paste it" is precisely the argument for a fenced code
block, so a sentence written to suppress fencing supplied its justification instead. A prohibition
should carry a reason that argues *against* the banned form, or none at all. Rate of fenced replies
went from one of three to three of three across the change; n is small and the mechanism is
inferred from the wording, so the causal claim is `[unverified]`, but the candidate earned no
promotion on its measured result regardless.

## Instrument correction — the fencing was an unstated rule (EVAL-006 option a)

`[verified]` With the skill left exactly as dispositioned above, the *scenario prompt* was changed
to state the constraint its grader already enforced: the JSON packet must be the only fenced code
block, other commands written inline. No grader predicate changed. On `123b867b` (CLI 2.1.251,
Sonnet, 3 trials, run `20260829T222151Z-02287972`): **2/3, fence clause 3/3.** Zero fence failures,
against seven in nine under the old wording.

That is the decisive result for this slice's attribution. The fence failures that dominated all
three earlier runs — and that both no-skill clean-room models also produced — were the instrument
testing a rule its prompt never gave, not the cut and not the skill. The rejected candidate above
was therefore built against a misdiagnosis; the revert stands on its measured verdict, but the
premise it was patching was the prompt's, not the body's.

The single remaining failure is a smaller and genuine finding: **label form, not posture.** Trial 1
wrote "(unverified until step 4/5 output exists)" — the correct epistemic posture — but not the
bracketed `[unverified]` token that AGENTS.md's evidence-label contract requires and that any
label-scanning consumer looks for. The grader is right to reject it.

`[verified]` **Confirmed at nine trials.** Six further Sonnet trials on `dec6bc94` scored 4/6 (run
`20260829T223656Z-69eab0e9`), pooling to **6/9**. Prompt, graders, split, threshold and the
`SKILL.md` blob `c7cd4f89` were verified byte-identical across both revisions before the second run,
so the pool is one measurement rather than two. **Routing 9/9. Fence clause 9/9.**

All three failures are the same mechanism: the model attaches a justification to the label, either
in parentheses or inside the brackets — `(unverified until step 4/5 output exists)`,
`[unverified — no log/describe output]`, `(unverified — no logs actually ...)`. The posture is
right in every case; only the form fails, and the form is what a label-scanning consumer matches.

This slice does not patch it. Two considerations: the disposition above rests on byte reduction and
probe evidence, not on this scenario, and a third body edit in one session on a skill whose failure
was twice misdiagnosed earns a fresh checkpoint rather than momentum. The fix is carried by
`EVAL-006`, along with the arithmetic that rules out closing the item with a threshold instead: at a
true rate of 0.67 a 2-of-3 gate reds 26% of the time absent any regression, and the Wilson 95%
interval on 6/9 spans 0.35–0.88.

`[verified]` **Reframe — the fixture forbids the task, so the label miss is probably not a defect.**
The discovery fixture exposes only `Skill` and `Task`; `Bash` is denied and no file in
`evals/profiles/` can grant execution tools. The prompt's "inspect the service, revisions, and logs"
is impossible by construction, and seven of the nine trials open by saying so. The scenario grades
the degraded path — what the lane says when it cannot investigate — not the triage lane its
`success_criteria` describe. On that path the reason for unverifiability is the most informative
thing in the reply, which is precisely what the three failing labels supplied. Two conclusions this
record had earlier stated too strongly are corrected here: "the grader is right to reject it" holds
only in the sense that the grader is self-consistent with a fleet-wide convention, and no runtime
consumer scans model output for the bare token (`validate_fleet.py`'s `EVIDENCE_TRIAD` checks
authored files); and the recommendation to patch the body for label form is withdrawn, because it
would tune the skill against a constraint no real caller imposes.

`[verified]` **What the probes say the screen does not.** Both tiers produced the entire Cloud Run
mechanics unprompted, so the content the models cannot supply is the guard decisions, the routing
lines, the unratified boundary and the answer contract. This slice stopped at −15% because the
entrypoint cleared 7,500 bytes, not because the probe said the remainder had earned its bytes. The
generalized rule is recorded as the SKILL-001 method note; for this skill it means a further cut is
supportable and was simply not taken here.
