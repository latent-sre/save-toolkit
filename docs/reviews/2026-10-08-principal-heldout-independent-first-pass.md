## 0. Reading of the fixture (what every record had to handle)

- Three writers (`rules-api` service API insert/update/hard-delete; `operator-cli` direct SQL insert/update; `cleanup-task` direct SQL hard-delete) and two readers (`routing-api` read-after-acknowledged-write; `rule-audit` current rules plus a separate audit history). The proposed redirect moves only `rules-api`'s binding; the other two writers hold their own credentials and schedules.
- `version` is per-rule only, hard delete removes row and version, no journal/tombstone/CDC/cross-DB transaction exists, local transactions do. No new messaging service.
- Rehearsal: a 2700 s snapshot copy with an unconditional upsert ends with source {r17 west v8, r22 blue v3} and target {r17 east v7, r18 green v1}: equal counts, stale update, resurrected delete, lost insert. A post-cutover target write r17 north v9 is the rollback hazard. `notes.md` (untrusted) proposes count-compare, API-only redirect and snapshot replay with `last_rule_id`.
- Constraints: 60 s maximum write pause at final cutover, reads continue, no acknowledged write lost across cutover *and recovery*, no ID reuse, no resurrection, rollback within 24 h preserving replacement-acknowledged writes. Daily snapshots only, no restore drill.

All six records converge on the same architecture: trigger-written change journal in the legacy database (same local transaction), delete tombstones, version- or sequence-guarded idempotent apply replacing the unconditional upsert, database-level fence at cutover, drain, flip all five parties, reverse journal from replacement to legacy for the 24 h window, native logical replication as a platform-confirmed substitute, and a blocked cutover stage pending trigger rights, a restore drill and a measured pause. The case therefore does not discriminate on mechanism choice; it discriminates on boundary rigor, edge-case coverage and constraint handling. Scores below reflect that.

## 2. Severity-ranked findings

Severity: high = constraint or data-loss path with no mitigation; medium = real gap mitigated elsewhere in the record; low = small or self-flagged.

1. **H01, C3, material defect, low, confidence high.** L194 "Abort on a pause above 60s." and L206 "A timed pause rehearsal, with the abort threshold at 60s." The abort decision is taken when the stopwatch passes the approved maximum, and the abort itself (restore grants, resume) adds seconds. Consequence: every aborted attempt violates Morgan's 60 s maximum by the abort duration. Smallest correction: set the abort deadline at a measured margin below 60 s (the other five records use 40-45 s or an owner-set figure below the limit) so the abort completes inside the budget.
2. **H02, C2, low note (not scored as a defect), confidence medium.** L59 "take a repeatable-read snapshot and record the journal `seq` position in the same transaction … replay the journal from that recorded position (overlap harmless)" and L54 "re-read with an overlap window; both are safe because apply is idempotent." A transaction that wrote its journal row (low `seq`) and commits after the snapshot is invisible to both the snapshot and the recorded position; replay from that position skips it unless the unquantified overlap reaches back far enough. Idempotency makes re-reading safe, not skipping. Mitigation present: stage 3 full row-level passes L87 and the fenced final drain close the gap. Smallest correction: start replay from the journal's first entry or from the transaction-visibility horizon H02 itself names in L54, and drop "both are safe".
3. **H01, C2, low, confidence medium.** L138-140 the applier "re-reads with an overlap margin" with the margin's sufficiency `[unverified]`. A long-open direct-SQL session can commit a low `seq` after the margin. Mitigated by continuous reconciliation and the fenced drain; self-flagged with a required concurrency test. Smallest correction: adopt a transaction-visibility (xmin/xid) horizon as H04 L61, H05 L84 and H06 L96 do.
4. **H01 and H06, C2/C4, low, confidence medium (ID minting is not in the fixture).** Neither record says how the replacement will mint rule IDs so that "never reused" holds after cutover, nor how legacy's allocator skips target-issued IDs after rollback. If minting is a sequence seeded from `max(rule_id)` of live rows, a deleted high ID is reissued and blocked by a tombstone (availability) or, outside the applier path, inserted (contract violation). H02 L60, H03 L98/L135, H04 L66 and H05 D8 address it. Smallest correction: add a stage-0 question on ID generation and a cutover step advancing the allocator beyond the highest ID ever issued, both directions.
5. **H04, C3, low, confidence medium.** L76 "Flip reads and writes in the same window, never reads first, otherwise a just-acked write may not be visible." During the fenced window nothing is acknowledged, so flipping readers before writers is the safe order (H03 L116, L145-147; H06 L119). The sentence reads as a warning against flipping readers before the window, which is right, but an implementer could invert the in-window order and create the opposite race (a write acknowledged on the target not visible to a reader still on legacy). L113 "Gate rules-api writes and fence legacy for CLI/cleanup" should also state that the DB fence covers the `rules-api` role. Smallest correction: reword L76 to "flip readers after the drain and before writers reopen" and name all three roles in the fence.
6. **H01, C4, low, confidence high.** L195 rollback "fence the target, drain the reverse journal into legacy, reconcile, repoint" omits unfencing legacy (fenced since stage 4 L194). As written the repointed writers are rejected. Smallest correction: add "unfence legacy" after repoint.
7. **H03, C5, accuracy note, low, confidence medium (depends on fixture line numbering matching the case-facts rendering).** Precise-looking `[verified: file:line]` cites are off in places: L47 "current-behavior.md:1-12" (11 lines), L76 "(lines 4-5, 14, 18)" (r17 snapshot is line 5), L78 "(lines 11, 17)" (source r22 is line 15), L119 "current-behavior.md:11-12" (10-11). Content is attributed to the right files and the design does not depend on the numbers. Smallest correction: cite files without line numbers unless they are checked.
8. **H01, C5, low, confidence high.** L194 names "BMC Remedy and/or Jira, whichever governs" as the change-record system; neither appears in the fixture. Hedged, so not a claimed integration. Smallest correction: "the team's change-record system".
9. **H02, C1, attribution note, pending trace.** Owners and operations for all three writers and the audit reader's requirement (L68-72) exist only in `inventory/*.csv`, yet L9 lists only `requirements.md`, `contracts/store.json`, `ops/current-behavior.md` as sources and the CSVs are never named. Not a false claim; whether the CSVs were read is a trace question.
10. **H01 and H02, C4, low.** Neither asks whether the 60 s pause also bounds a rollback; H03 D3, H04 D2, H05 D3 and H06 D2 do. Smallest correction: add the decision.

No high- or medium-severity finding. No record proposes count comparison, a blind fixed delay, snapshot replay, API-only redirect, or claims that any mechanism, integration, approval or test exists or ran.

## 3. Important strengths (by ID, substantive only)

- **H03** L104: control-table fence where writer triggers take a shared lock and the fence flip takes an exclusive lock, so the flip waits for in-flight commits and every later write errors; this gives a provable drain boundary rather than "wait a bit". L98 and L135: ID allocator advanced beyond the highest ID ever issued in both directions. L130: a trigger error aborts the user write (not acknowledged) and must be tested. L165: automatic writer retries after cutover must not duplicate or reuse an ID.
- **H06** L96: applier reads only rows whose `xid` is below the oldest in-flight transaction. L105: `session_replication_role` or privileged sessions bypass triggers, so the grants review must show no writer holds that privilege. L75: `TRUNCATE` must be rejected or journalled. L119: explicit readers-then-writers order with a lossless abort point. L70: states and rejects the simpler no-reverse-path design against the rollback requirement.
- **H05** L82-93: a justified alternative to version guards. Per-rule `seq` equals commit order via the row lock, apply only if `seq` exceeds the rule's `last_seq`, replay from the journal's first entry, copier reduced to insert-if-absent honoring tombstones. It has no start-position gap and does not depend on every direct-SQL update bumping `version`. L72: a key missing on the target cannot be classified as not-yet-inserted versus deleted without a tombstone or journal. L105: pre-staged PCF instance bound to the target plus a route flip, avoiding a restage inside the pause.
- **H04** L61: snapshot-horizon read by `txid` versus `xmin`. L36: the rehearsal's r17 v7 to v9 jump with v8 never present. L66: `max(rule_id)` is not sufficient for the allocator. L127: the journal is inside the same database and is not an independent copy, so stage 7 stays blocked. D7 L151: confirm version increases on every direct-SQL update (the version guard's hidden assumption). D5 L149: hold-versus-reject with about 3,000 queued writes.
- **H02** L59: explicit install-journal, snapshot, copy, replay ordering with concurrent copy and replay shown safe. L61: grants revoked from all three writer roles so PCF instances restarting on old bindings fail closed. L99: the reverse-synced legacy copy is not a substitute for a restore drill.
- **H01** L92-94: a cleanup deletion applied only to legacy lets a rule reappear on rollback or re-copy. L174: a role bypassing triggers or a `TRUNCATE` as a capture gap. L124-126: application pauses are insufficient because the CLI and cleanup hold their own credentials. Decision 4 L28-29: puts the meaning of "survive recovery" to Morgan with the RPO consequence stated.
- **All six**: the equal-count counterexample is explained with the three concrete rows; `notes.md` is rejected on the rehearsal's own data; the daily-snapshot recovery gap is made a blocking prerequisite rather than papered over; `last_rule_id` is correctly described as a copier resume cursor.
