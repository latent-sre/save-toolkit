## GCP-17 hardened — Remittance subscription backlog

Profile: cli; partition: tuning; group: managed-services; read budget: 3.
Source data: [family JSON](hardened/gcp17-h.json).

Competing lead: Identical, attractive reads point each variant toward the wrong mechanism. Toward poison messages (wrong in A): subscriber-errors shows recurring 'message nacked for redelivery' ledger-409 entries, one message ID nacked twice at 13:02, and nacks on SIGTERM at the 12:53 release, while subscriber-revision shows release 5.8.0 accepting only schema version 6. Toward insufficient capacity (wrong in B): subscriber-service shows the subscriber fixed at two instances, the change calendar puts release 5.8.0 about two minutes before applied throughput halved, and backlog and oldest age rising together match Pub/Sub's documented 'subscribers not keeping up' pattern. Only subscriber-deliveries (handler time against eight threads; repeated message IDs) and subscription-metrics (oldest-age slope of 0.5 versus 1.0 s/s) separate the mechanisms; in C both return no usable observation, so neither mechanism is supported.

### Variant A

Discriminating reads: `subscription-metrics`, `subscriber-deliveries`

Subscriber processing capacity is below the arrival rate. Every sampled delivery takes about 2.3-2.5 s, and eight handler threads (two fixed instances with four threads each) at about 2.4 s complete about 8 / 2.4 x 60 = 200 messages per minute, matching the ~200 applied per minute against ~400 arriving; the ~200-per-minute shortfall equals the backlog growth of 1,000 per 5 minutes. The sample agrees: 49 acked deliveries in about 296 s at 1 in 20 IDs is about 199 per minute, so the threads are saturated. Oldest unacked age rises 150 s per 300 s (0.5 s/s), less than elapsed time, so the oldest messages keep being acknowledged and no message has stayed unacknowledged since the 12:55 onset. The ERROR-log nacks are ledger 409 conflicts at about 0.4 per minute both before and after 12:55, and the one sampled conflict (16429770318845207) was acked on redelivery 13.7 s later; they cannot account for a 200-per-minute shortfall. Why the handler now takes about 2.4 s, when the same hour yesterday applied ~400 per minute, is not established; release 5.8.0 and ledger latency are candidates, not demonstrated causes.

**Useful next checks:**

- The remit-applier application owner breaks down handler time (ledger call versus local processing) for 5.8.0 against 5.7.4 and the previous day before proposing any thread, instance or rollback change through ITO.
- The ledger owner checks ledger write latency and conflict rates since 12:50 UTC and confirms whether more concurrent writers are safe before any capacity increase.
- Priya tracks applied rate, backlog and oldest unacked age over later complete intervals; recovery requires the applied rate to exceed arrivals and the age to fall, not one good sample. The ledger owner reconciles applied remittance IDs before any separate replay or disposition decision.

**Unsupported conclusions/actions:**

- Diagnosing poison or unsupported messages from the ERROR-log nacks, the repeated conflict at 13:02 or ACCEPTED_SCHEMA_VERSIONS=6, which the delivery sample and the age slope contradict.

**Unknowns to preserve:**

## GCP-25 hardened — Index-publisher release Pod not becoming ready

Profile: cli; partition: tuning; group: gke; read budget: 3.

**Sources:**

- [After a container exits, the kubelet restarts it with an exponential back-off (10s, 20s, 40s, ...) capped at 300 seconds, and resets it after 10 minutes of running; CrashLoopBackOff means that back-off is in effect for a container that keeps failing.](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/workloads/pods/pod-lifecycle/) — checked 2026-10-17.
- [ImagePullBackOff means a container could not start because its image could not be pulled; Kubernetes retries with an increasing back-off up to 300 seconds. A tag other than latest with no explicit pull policy gets IfNotPresent.](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/containers/images/) — checked 2026-10-17.
- [CrashLoopBackOff means containers repeatedly start and exit, with delays growing up to five minutes; a non-zero exit points at the application or its configuration, and OOMKilled or liveness-probe restarts are separate causes to confirm.](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/troubleshooting/crashloopbackoff-events) — checked 2026-10-17.
- [ImagePullBackOff means the kubelet failed to pull the image and keeps retrying, up to five minutes apart; pull events are found with log_id("events") and resource.type="k8s_pod". Kubelet pulls from Artifact Registry use the node VM's service account, not Workload Identity Federation for GKE.](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/troubleshooting/image-pulls) — checked 2026-10-17.
- [Kubernetes events are in the projects/PROJECT_ID/logs/events log under k8s_pod, k8s_node or k8s_cluster depending on the emitting object; container stdout and stderr use k8s_container with the stdout and stderr log names.](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/how-to/view-logs) — checked 2026-10-17.
- [Cluster events are removed after one hour; a kube-system deployment collects events and sends them to Logging on a best-effort basis; container logs are removed when their host Pod is removed.](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/about-logs) — checked 2026-10-17.
- [k8s_container carries project_id, location, cluster_name, namespace_name, pod_name and container_name; k8s_pod the same without container_name; k8s_node carries node_name; build carries project_id, build_id and build_trigger_id.](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/api/v2/resource-list) — checked 2026-10-17.
